# Day 10 — (title not explicitly stated in transcript) (Sep 16)

## Core teaching
- A "different brain" doesn't mean a brain that never experiences doubt — it means you have a different relationship with doubt when it arrives. You stop turning one funky day into a prophecy about your future.
- Personal growth is an upward spiral, not a straight line. You can feel unstoppable Monday and question everything Wednesday. Progress is meeting the same fear with more evidence, more self-trust, and faster recovery — not a perfectly ascending belief score.
- Brainwashing means deliberately strengthening the thoughts you want to be able to return to. When the wobble comes, you quiet the noise, put your blinders back on, and ask: "What is the next most important step?"
- Don't mistake a wobble for moving backwards. Don't shrink your desires just because they feel emotionally unsafe. You're allowed to want the bigger house, the money, the best-selling book, the million followers.
- 10 days was never the deadline — it was the beginning. The practice continues: morning hypnosis before your phone, journaling, messy action, and borrowing belief from the group when yours dips.

## Exercises & assignments
- **Re-rate your belief:** "From one to 10, how much do you actually believe your delusional goal is possible?" Write down today's number and compare it to your day zero number.
- **Review your day zero writing:** Go back and read what you wrote on day zero — the sentences you used around your goal. Look at what you wrote yesterday about your flops. Look at the actions you've taken over the last 10 days. Look at the things you're willing to admit you want now.
- **Celebrate your actions:** "Take a moment to really celebrate all these actions that you took."
- **Closing ritual with your goal:** "Look at the goal you wrote down on day zero, the one that you put under the candle, and ask, 'Does this still scare me?'" Decide whether to keep it, resize it, or rewrite it.
- **Rewrite your goal on a fresh page:** "Write, 'My delusional goal is...' and then write it in the present tense. And write the version you actually want. Not the version that sounds reasonable, not the version you think you can guarantee, not the version that you think would be least embarrassing if you failed."
- **Four things for today:**
  1. Look at what your number changed to, from day zero to day ten.
  2. Look at your goal again and decide if you want to keep it, resize it, rewrite it — "whatever feels true."
  3. "If you wrote consistency, do the rep. If you wrote courage, do the scary thing. If you wrote structure, create the structure. If you wrote patience, stop digging up the seed every 12 minutes to check whether it grew."
  4. "Tomorrow morning, before your phone. Brainwash yourself."
- **If belief dips after the challenge:** Go back into the group, read the wins, read the goals, and borrow belief until you have enough evidence to hold your own again.
