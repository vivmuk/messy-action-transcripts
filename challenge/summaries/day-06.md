# Day 6 — Finding the Gremlin Agendas (Sep 12)

## Core teaching
- There are two levels of belief: the conscious level (what you'd say out loud) and the subconscious level (what's running in the background and actually driving your decisions).
- The subconscious protects its patterns — it doesn't care whether a program helps or sabotages you. Its number one agenda is safety, and "safe" means "familiar."
- There's a "glass door" hypnotists call the critical faculty that fact-checks every new idea against what you already believe. If it doesn't match, it gets tossed out — which is why affirmations, journaling, and vision boards only get you so far.
- Hidden agendas disguise themselves as humility, realism, or "now is not a good time." Underneath, it's a protector running a program you never chose.
- The door lowers in a hyper-suggestible state (like when you jump at a scary movie even though you know it's fiction). In a light trance, the window between conscious and subconscious narrows, and rehearsing your goal in first person lets the belief slip in.
- The shift comes early (around day 10) but the new program becomes the default much later. The students who got mind-blowing results were the ones still lowering the door on day 30 after the novelty wore off.

## Exercises & assignments
- Open your journal and write your delusional goal at the top of a fresh page.
- Finish this sentence: "If I actually got this, the bad thing that might happen is ___" or "here's why I might not actually want this." Be honest, the way you were on day five with your fears — but this time asking what part of you is afraid of *getting* it, not of not getting it.
- Write as many as you can think of. Treat it as a giant brainstorm.
- Next to each one, write what the gremlin thinks it's protecting you from (e.g., "being judged," "being seen," "disappointing my mom," "finding out I'm not good enough").
- Turn each one into an "I remember when" statement in past tense, like day zero and day four. Example: "I remember when part of me believed that making money would make me a worse person. Now I understand that my niece has nothing about my character and that the gremlin was trying to protect a reputation that nobody was threatening." *(Note: "my niece" appears to be a transcription error for "money" in context.)*
- Do your brainwashing (self-hypnosis track).
- Come into the group chat and share one messy action you've taken since joining — something the version of you from a week ago would have put off.
- Before posting, check in with yourself: "How do you feel in comparison to just six days ago? Are you believing more of what's possible for you? Are you spiraling even just a little bit less, taking action on that scary thing just a little sooner?"
- Reflect on every mindset shift you've noticed so far.
- Show someone else some support in the group chat.
