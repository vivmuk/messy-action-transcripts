# Day 2 — Magic (Sep 8)

## Core teaching
- Manifestation is defined simply: "the process of taking [something] that exists first as an idea or desire in your mind and making it real in the physical world" — creating something out of nothing, not a get-rich-quick scheme.
- An extraordinary life is "a life built by design that feels extraordinarily right for you" — not necessarily private jets or fame; it could be a camper van or a tiny ceramic studio.
- The recipe for creating it: Clarity + Belief + Aligned Messy Action (C + B + A). Clarity comes from Time With Self plus micro actions on what you already know; belief is why we "brainwash"; aligned action is where "the free rides come in."
- Your gremlins have competing desires — usually safety — and will unconsciously steer your actions toward the outcome that feels safest, not the one you consciously say you want.
- Critical distinction: "You do not manifest your trauma." Manifestation is about increasing your ability to intentionally participate in creating your life, not controlling everything that happens to you.
- Skepticism and closed-mindedness are different; skepticism never hurt her, but refusing to consider things she couldn't fully explain would have cost her the best things in her life.

## Exercises & assignments
- Take your delusional goal and run it through C + B + A.
- **Clarity:** "Ask yourself… What do I actually want? Forget what would impress people, what makes the most sense on paper, or what you think you should want." Also: "What do I no longer want to keep doing? What do I know for sure I don't want in my life?" — "make those two lists."
- **Belief:** Notice "Where does your brain immediately tell you that that desire is impossible, irresponsible, embarrassing, unrealistic, or unsafe." Just catch the gremlins in the act; you don't need to solve every belief today.
- **Aligned messy action:** Ask, "What is one nudge I already have that I keep ignoring?" and "What is just one small reversible messy action that you can take to test that nudge?" Examples she gives: "Send the email, research the city, post the idea, book the class, write the first page, have the conversation, run the experiment."

## Key quotes
