# Day 7 — Troubleshooting & Capacity (Sep 13)

*Note: No explicit title is given in the transcript; this is a descriptive title based on the episode's content.*

## Core teaching
- Four logistical reasons the hypnosis track may not feel like it's "working" yet: (1) expecting fireworks instead of recognizing a light trance just feels like relaxed heaviness, (2) being physically present but mentally distracted by to-do lists or constantly checking "am I in yet?", (3) doing the track right after checking phone/email/social media so your brain is already scattered, and (4) only doing three minutes when you should work toward 10–20.
- The fifth reason — the one that has "nothing to do with the track at all" — is burnout and lack of capacity. "A burnt out brain cannot think delusionally." You can do the practice perfectly, but if you're running on five hours of sleep with zero white space and saying yes to everything, your goal will feel like one more thing on the pile.
- There's a critical distinction between *discomfort* (which you should lean into — posting the video, having the sales conversation) and *genuinely overriding your capacity* (which requires rest and boundaries). "Am I resisting this because it's uncomfortable? Or am I overriding myself because I genuinely don't have the capacity? Those require two completely different responses."
- Following small intuitive nudges creates "shortcuts" and "free rides" — the magic of alignment. Examples: her best posts come from downloads on walks with no music; her boyfriend's brother randomly messaged a girl whose apartment he'd toured years ago, and they've now been dating over a year.
- Internal shifts tend to arrive around day 10, but results operate on their own timeline — her 100K followers took nine months; her 21K new followers this summer took 4–5 weeks of daily posting *after* the internal shift. "Most delusional goals require playing the long game."

## Exercises & assignments
- **Capacity dilution audit**: Look at your goal and ask, "What is currently eating the space that this goal needs?" Write down everything that comes up (e.g., the 11 PM scroll, five hours of sleep, the yes you wanted to be a no, the guilt-kept project).
- For each item, ask: "Is this actually draining my capacity Or is it simply uncomfortable?" — Grace warns against using "capacity" as a sophisticated new excuse to avoid messy action.
- Ask: "What is one boundary I'm putting in place this week to get some of that space back?" Just one. Examples she gives: bed by a certain time, using an app blocker like Jomo, saying no to something, pausing a project, asking for help.
- Ask: "What am I willing to protect for this goal even when it's inconvenient?" (sleep, first 20 minutes of the morning, workout, two hours of creative time)
- If putting a boundary in place feels terrifying, write down the gremlin that shows up — e.g., "What if you disappoint them? They'll think I'm selfish. I should be able to handle all of this. I don't deserve to rest until everything gets done." Examine the program underneath it.
- **Resentment audit**: Ask where you're feeling resentful. Then ask, "Is there anywhere I have more power to change the situation than I've been giving myself credit for? What conversation do you need to have? What do you need to ask for? What do you need to say no to?"
- Keep asking yourself the question from an earlier day: "What would bring me joy today?"
- Come into the group and share what boundary you're putting in place. While there, show someone else some love, "especially the people sharing something vulnerable."

## Key quotes
- "A burnt out brain cannot think delusionally."
- "There is no award at the end for whoever suffered the most while achieving their goal."
- "The calendar doesn't clear itself. You clear it one boundary at a time, and then you put the goal in the space that you made."
- "Your goal doesn't just need more hours thrown at it. It needs a version of you who actually has the capacity to hold it."
